# Writing for the site

How the words on this site should sound: pages, labels, hints, error messages and emails.
Written 7 November 2026 after Tori (a volunteer testing on her phone) and others said that, in
several places, it was obvious an AI had written the text. **Read this before writing any
words a family, volunteer or staff member will see.** Code comments are not covered by it.

## The voice

Imagine a friendly person in the camp office, here in East Tennessee, helping a parent fill in a
form over the phone. They are warm and plain, and they don't waste the parent's time. They say
what to do next. They don't explain how the computer works, and they don't make a show of being
honest or careful.

- **American spelling.** Honor, color, behavior, favor, center, organize, program, check (not
  cheque).
- **Short sentences.** One idea each. If a sentence needs a dash, a semicolon and a bracket, it is
  two or three sentences.
- **Say what to do, then why if it helps.** For example: "Pay the $50 deposit so staff can
  approve your registration."
- **Use "you", "we" and contractions:** you'll, we'll, don't.
- **Name things the way families do:** "camp", "the week", "your family", "your dashboard". Avoid
  "registration participant", "record", "submission" and "the system".

## What gives it away as AI. Don't write:

- **Em dashes (—) as the all-purpose joiner.** This is the biggest tell. Use a period, a comma or
  a colon. Keep a dash only where nothing else reads as clearly, and that should be rare. In
  short labels, write "No, I've been before", not "No — been before".
- **Announcing your own honesty or care:** "Being straight with you", "To be clear",
  "Honestly", "We want to be upfront", "Rest assured".
- **Explaining the plumbing:** "The application attaches to a registration", "that paperwork
  happens separately, never on this site", "nothing else about your registration changes", "this
  is saved to your household for next time" (unless the family needs to know it to act).
- **Pre-emptive reassurance and permission-giving:** "X is fine", "'None' is an answer", "and
  that's okay", "no pressure", "feel free", "don't hesitate to".
- **Clever or writerly phrasing:** "with real numbers", "in the same pass", "what's already in
  place", "a heads-up", "the whole picture".
- **Neat groups of three, stacked asides and brackets inside brackets.**
- **Filler words:** simply, just, seamless, ensure, journey, easily, quickly, "a few short
  questions".
- **Labels that carry a paragraph:** "(required — 'none' is an answer)". Write the label, mark
  it required, and if a hint is really needed, put one short line under the box.

## What not to change while rewording

- **Facts and decisions.** Amounts, dates, who decides what, what is required, and what staff
  will do. Change the wording, not the policy.
- **Anything stored or compared in the code:** `<option value>`, the volunteer-area names (they
  are saved on applications), role and status words used as keys, and agreement text (it lives in
  the database and changes only as a new version).
- **Wording that a comment says was set by a named person, word for word** (for example "verbatim"
  or "Larry's words"). Leave it, or make only the spelling American.
